## Why technical problems cost more on an online store

A blog with a crawl issue loses a few articles. A store with the same issue can lose whole categories. E-commerce sites generate URLs faster than anyone can review them: filters, sort orders, session parameters, out-of-stock variants, internal search pages. Left alone, Google spends its crawl on those duplicates while the pages that carry margin get crawled late, indexed partially or not at all.

The symptoms look like a content or competition problem. Category pages slide from position 4 to 11. New products take weeks to appear. Search Console shows "Crawled, currently not indexed" growing every month. Core Web Vitals pass on the home page and fail on every product template. None of this shows up in a monthly traffic chart until revenue has already dropped.

## What this audit does differently

We start with the money. Before crawling, we pull organic revenue by landing page so every issue can be weighed against the traffic and orders it touches. A redirect chain on ten dead URLs goes to the bottom of the list. A canonical bug on the template behind 4,000 product pages goes to the top.

Then we look at the site the way Google does: raw HTML versus rendered HTML, internal link depth, how filters and pagination are handled, and what is actually sitting in the index. Where a store relies on [e-commerce SEO](/services/ecommerce-seo/) work such as new category pages, the audit confirms those pages can be found and indexed before anyone invests in content.

Sites planning a move get a migration baseline. If you are consolidating domains or redirecting acquired sites, the audit feeds directly into [domain strategy](/services/domain-strategy/) so no equity is lost. If you need someone to sit with your developers while fixes ship, that continues as [SEO consulting](/services/seo-consulting/).
