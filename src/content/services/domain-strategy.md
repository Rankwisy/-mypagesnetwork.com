## Where domain decisions go wrong

Domains carry history. Every link a site ever earned, every page Google indexed, every brand search it collected is tied to that hostname. Change the domain carelessly and much of that history is lost. Redirect a domain with a spam past and you can import someone else's problems.

The common failures are predictable. A rebrand where the old URLs all redirect to the new home page. A merger of four country sites where nobody mapped the product URLs. An expired domain bought for its metrics, redirected to an unrelated store, and quietly ignored by Google.

## How we handle domain strategy

We treat every domain like an acquisition. Before money changes hands or redirects go live, we read the archive history, check who links to the domain and why, look for ownership changes and spam patterns, and estimate what value is actually transferable. The free [Domain Overview](/tools/domain-overview/) tool gives a quick first look at a domain's traffic and top keywords.

The redirect map is where the value is kept or lost. Each URL with links or traffic goes to the closest relevant page on the destination site. Where no equivalent page exists, we decide whether to create one. This is detailed work, and it is the reason migrations we run tend to recover quickly.

Before a migration, a [technical SEO audit](/services/technical-seo-audit/) records the baseline and catches faults that would otherwise be carried to the new domain. Consolidated authority works best when paired with ongoing [link building](/services/link-building/), and complex multi-site decisions are often worked through first in [SEO consulting](/services/seo-consulting/) sessions with your leadership team.
