## When programmatic SEO is the right tool

Some markets are made of thousands of small queries rather than a few big ones: "accountant in Leeds", "Shopify vs WooCommerce for fashion", "replacement filter for model X200". Nobody can write a thousand pages by hand, and nobody should try. But if you hold the data that answers each of those queries, a well-designed template can.

The catch is that most programmatic projects fail the same way. They take a list of cities or keywords, swap them into one block of text, publish ten thousand pages and watch Google index two hundred of them. The pages are not wrong, they are just empty.

## How we build programmatic pages that get indexed

Everything starts with data. Before we design a template, we confirm that each page will contain something specific: local prices, inventory counts, ratings, specifications, statistics, comparisons. If the data is not there, we say so and suggest a smaller, hand-built set instead, usually through [content strategy](/services/content-strategy/).

The template is designed for the query, not for the database. A "[service] in [city]" page needs local proof and a clear next step. A "[product] vs [product]" page needs a comparison table and a verdict. We build a sample, review it by hand, then publish in batches and track indexing per batch.

Scale also multiplies technical faults. A bad canonical or a slow template is no longer one problem but ten thousand. That is why we often pair programmatic work with a [technical SEO audit](/services/technical-seo-audit/) of the target section. For stores, attribute and brand pages are a programmatic exercise in their own right, covered within [e-commerce SEO](/services/ecommerce-seo/).

To size a pattern before committing, check the volume of a few combinations in the free [Keyword Ideas](/tools/keyword-ideas/) tool and multiply by the number of pages your data supports.
