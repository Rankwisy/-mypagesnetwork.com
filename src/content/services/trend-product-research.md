## The cost of finding out too late

Most stores discover a trend from their own sales data, after competitors have already captured the search demand. By the time a product sells well enough to notice, the top category pages have been ranking for weeks and the paid search auction has become expensive.

Search data moves earlier. People search for a product before they buy it, often long before. Rising query volume, new modifiers ("for small spaces", "rechargeable", "eco") and changes in what Google shows on the results page all signal demand that has not yet reached your sales report.

## How we research products

We start with the constraints that matter to you: category, price band, markets, supply. There is no point surfacing a trend you cannot stock or ship. Within those limits, we scan rising queries across up to 20 markets and compare them. A product taking off in the Netherlands is often three months ahead of the same product in Spain or Italy.

Each candidate is scored on demand, growth, competition and fit. You can look at a product's numbers yourself with the free [Keyword Ideas](/tools/keyword-ideas/) tool, which shows volume and 12-month trend for any seed term.

Research is only useful if it turns into pages that rank in time. Chosen products feed straight into [e-commerce SEO](/services/ecommerce-seo/) for category placement and templates, and into [content strategy](/services/content-strategy/) for buying guides that capture early research queries. Where a trend covers many variations, such as sizes, compatibility or use cases, [programmatic SEO](/services/programmatic-seo/) can cover the long tail quickly.

The result is a short, ranked list with the evidence behind it, and a plan to be visible when demand peaks.
