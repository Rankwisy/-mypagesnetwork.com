## Content that ranks but does not sell

Many sites publish steadily and still see little commercial return. The blog brings visitors who read one article and leave. Articles target queries the business cannot serve. Ten old posts compete for the same keyword, so none of them ranks well. And the category and service pages, which actually make money, have no content support and no internal links pointing at them.

The opposite case is common too: commercial pages with a single line of text and no answer to the questions buyers ask before choosing.

## How we plan content

We plan backwards from revenue. Every topic in the map is tied to a commercial page it should support: a category, a product line or a service. If a topic cannot connect to something you sell, it needs a strong reason to be on the list, such as earning links.

We check what Google rewards before writing. The free [SERP Checker](/tools/serp-checker/) shows the live top 20 for any query: whether Google wants guides, lists, tools or product pages tells you what format to build. Store owners get category copy and buying guides inside [e-commerce SEO](/services/ecommerce-seo/), and large data-driven sets are handled through [programmatic SEO](/services/programmatic-seo/).

Content is also how you become a source for AI answers. Clear definitions, original data and well-structured comparisons are what AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity quote. That is the overlap with [AI search optimization](/services/ai-search-optimization/), and we plan for both at the same time.

The last step is maintenance. Pages decay as competitors update theirs. A refresh plan, prioritised by traffic lost, often brings back more visits in a quarter than a year of new publishing.
