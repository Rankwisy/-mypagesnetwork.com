## Why local search behaves differently

Local results are a separate game from regular organic rankings. The map pack weighs three things: how relevant your profile is to the query, how close you are to the searcher, and how prominent your business looks online. A beautiful website does not help much if the Google Business Profile has the wrong primary category, a different phone number than your directory listings, and twelve reviews against a competitor's three hundred.

Most businesses also check their rankings from their own office, which is the one place they are almost guaranteed to rank. Two kilometres away, the picture is often very different.

## How we run local SEO

We start by checking your rankings the way your customers see them. Our geo-grid tracking measures positions across your whole service area, and you can do a quick version yourself with the free [Google Search Simulator](/tools/google-search-simulator/) by switching country and language.

Next we fix the profile and the listings. Correct categories, services and attributes matter more than most owners realise. Consistent name, address and phone across directories confirms to Google that the business is real and where it is. Citation building and local links, part of our wider [link building](/services/link-building/) work, then add the prominence signals.

Location pages give each area or branch a page that can rank in organic results under the map pack. They need genuinely local content: services offered there, staff, directions, local proof. That editorial work follows the same [content strategy](/services/content-strategy/) process as the rest of the site.

Local queries are also where AI answers are spreading fastest. "Best dentist near me" increasingly returns a generated summary that cites a few businesses. Strong reviews and consistent data are what those systems draw from, which is why local work feeds directly into [AI search optimization](/services/ai-search-optimization/).
